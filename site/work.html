I created the Bitcoin privacy wallet, following my earlier HiddenWallet work, and led its early development. It brought CoinJoin, Tor, and private transaction discovery into one wallet. Bitcoin privacy protocols

ZeroLink: The Bitcoin Fungibility Framework
I created ZeroLink in 2017. It combines Chaumian CoinJoin with requirements for coin selection, transaction fees, and how a wallet treats coins before and after mixing. Those surrounding rules matter because a private mixing round can still be undone by later wallet behavior.
Initial commit · 2017 announcement
TumbleBit / NTumbleBit
Nicolas Dorier and I worked on an independent implementation in 2016–2017. My contributions included wallet integration, Tor support, configuration, and documentation. The original NDSS paper names us as implementers and thanks me for discussions and suggestions. I wrote a five-part series explaining the protocol and how to try the implementation.
TumbleBit paper · Understanding TumbleBit · TumbleBit vs CoinJoin
WabiSabi: Centrally Coordinated CoinJoins with Variable Amounts
I co-authored the paper with István András Seres, Yuval Kogman, and Lucas Ontivero. Anonymous credentials let participants register outputs of different amounts without revealing to the coordinator which inputs paid for them. Value commitments enforce the accounting. This is the protocol used in Wasabi 2.0.
Journal article, 22 October 2021 · March 2021 preprint
Pay To EndPoint (P2EP)
I co-proposed P2EP after a group discussion and published its explanation in July 2018. Sender and receiver both contribute inputs to a payment, disrupting the assumption that all inputs belong to the sender. Nicolas Dorier’s later BIP78 Payjoin proposal acknowledges my implementation feedback.
BIP78: A Simple Payjoin Proposal

Research, analysis, and discussion

Wasabi Research Club / Wasabi Research Experience

The archive includes meeting agendas, notes, slides, and recordings on CoinShuffle, SNICKER, CashFusion, anonymous credentials, and WabiSabi.
Recordings · CoinShuffle++ discussion
Dumplings and CoinJoin analysis
I built Dumplings in 2020 to derive reproducible CoinJoin statistics from Bitcoin blockchain data. It separates newly mixed coins from remixes and examines change and post-mix consolidation. This follows my earlier CoinJoin-efficiency and Wasabi-versus-Whirlpool analyses. The academic study Adoption and Actual Privacy of Decentralized CoinJoin Implementations in Bitcoin builds on and evaluates these detection heuristics.
Earlier efficiency analysis · Wasabi and Whirlpool · Transaction-analysis notes and code · Academic study
CoinJoin amount design / Sake
Allowing different input amounts creates a practical question: how should those amounts be split into outputs? I wrote amount-organization simulations in 2020 and began Sake in December 2021, with later contributions from the team. It compares denomination strategies, equal outputs, change, fees, and block-space use. Dumplings supplies parameters from real transactions; my Wasabi 2.0 launch log compares the simulations with mainnet output decompositions.
2020 simulations · June 2022 launch log · Privacy guarantees and amount decomposition

Earlier wallets and libraries

DotNetWallet / HiddenWallet
I wrote a cross-platform command-line wallet for the 2016 tutorial Build a Bitcoin wallet in C#. It covered wallet creation and recovery, receiving, balance and transaction history, and sending. That work developed into HiddenWallet, the precursor to Wasabi. I also contributed to the early Breeze TumbleBit wallet in 2017.
Breeze · 2018 account of the wallet projects
HiddenBitcoin / HBitcoin
My privacy-oriented Bitcoin libraries built on Nicolas Dorier’s NBitcoin. They supported wallet and transaction handling, including full-block SPV wallet work. These are part of the history leading to HiddenWallet and Wasabi.
HiddenBitcoin
DotNetTor
I built this .NET Core library for sending HTTP traffic through Tor’s SOCKS interface and managing Tor circuits. It supported my wallet and NTumbleBit work.
Published packages
Wallet network privacy
I investigated how a lightweight wallet can discover its transactions without telling a server which addresses it owns. My writing examines full-block downloads, client-side filtering, Tor, and their privacy tradeoffs.
