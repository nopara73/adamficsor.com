Wasabi Wallet and zkSNACKs

Bitcoin’s transaction history is public. CoinJoin combines several people’s coins in one transaction to make ownership harder to trace.

Wasabi Wallet
I created this Bitcoin privacy wallet and led its early development. It grew out of HiddenWallet, combining CoinJoin, Tor, and ways to find transactions without sharing the wallet’s addresses with a server. Wasabi 1.0 launched on 31 October 2018; Wasabi 2.0 followed in June 2022 with the WabiSabi protocol.
Version 1.0 · Source · The Wasabi 2.0 launch

Bitcoin privacy protocols

ZeroLink: The Bitcoin Fungibility Framework
I created ZeroLink in 2017. It describes the mixing protocol used in the original Wasabi Wallet and the wallet rules around it: coin selection, fees, and how coins are handled before and after a mix. Those rules help prevent later transactions from undoing the privacy gained by mixing.
Initial commit · 2017 announcement
TumbleBit / NTumbleBit
Nicolas Dorier and I worked on NTumbleBit in 2016–2017, implementing the TumbleBit protocol for private Bitcoin payments. I worked on wallet integration, Tor support, configuration, and documentation, and wrote the five-part Understanding TumbleBit series.
TumbleBit paper · Understanding TumbleBit · TumbleBit vs CoinJoin
WabiSabi: Centrally Coordinated CoinJoins with Variable Amounts
I co-authored this paper with István András Seres, Yuval Kogman, and Lucas Ontivero. The protocol lets people join a transaction with different amounts while hiding the links between their inputs and outputs from the coordinator. Wasabi 2.0 uses it.
Journal article, 22 October 2021 · March 2021 preprint
Pay To EndPoint (P2EP)
I co-proposed P2EP in 2018 and wrote the design explanation. Both sender and receiver contribute coins to a payment, breaking the usual assumption that all its inputs belong to the sender. I later gave implementation feedback on Nicolas Dorier’s BIP78 Payjoin proposal.
BIP78: A Simple Payjoin Proposal

Bitcoin privacy research

Wasabi Research Club
I helped start these public discussions of Bitcoin privacy research in 2020. We read papers and discussed them with their authors before moving on to our own WabiSabi research. The archive has agendas, recordings, notes, and slides.
Recordings · CoinShuffle++ discussion
Dumplings and CoinJoin analysis
I built Dumplings in 2020 to study CoinJoin activity on the blockchain: new coins, repeat mixes, change, and coins later spent together. It continued my earlier comparisons of Wasabi and Whirlpool. Researchers later used and evaluated these methods in a study of CoinJoin adoption and privacy.
Earlier efficiency analysis · Wasabi and Whirlpool · Transaction-analysis notes and code · Study of CoinJoin adoption and privacy
Sake: choosing CoinJoin amounts
I started Sake in 2021 to simulate how CoinJoin wallets choose output amounts. With later contributions from the Wasabi team, it tests the tradeoffs between privacy, fees, and transaction size. It builds on my 2020 simulations and uses data from Dumplings.
2020 simulations · June 2022 launch log · Privacy guarantees and amount decomposition

Earlier wallets and libraries

DotNetWallet / HiddenWallet
I wrote DotNetWallet, a command-line wallet, for my 2016 tutorial Build a Bitcoin wallet in C#. It developed into HiddenWallet, the precursor to Wasabi. I also contributed to the Breeze TumbleBit wallet in 2017.
Breeze · 2018 account of the wallet projects
HiddenBitcoin / HBitcoin
Bitcoin libraries I wrote on top of Nicolas Dorier’s NBitcoin for handling wallets and transactions. They supported my early wallet work, including downloading full blocks to check transactions locally.
HiddenBitcoin
DotNetTor
I built this .NET library to send HTTP traffic through Tor and manage Tor circuits. I used it in my wallet and NTumbleBit work.
