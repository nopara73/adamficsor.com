Protocols, papers, and a book

ZeroLink: The Bitcoin Fungibility Framework
I created ZeroLink in 2017. It combines a Chaumian CoinJoin design with wallet behavior and privacy requirements.
Initial commit, 28 July 2017
WabiSabi: Centrally Coordinated CoinJoins with Variable Amounts
Co-authored with István András Seres, Yuval Kogman, and Lucas Ontivero. The protocol uses anonymous credentials and value commitments to enable CoinJoins with variable amounts.
Cryptoeconomic Systems, volume 1, issue 2, 22 October 2021. Journal article · March 2021 preprint
Programming the Blockchain in C#
Originally written by Nicolas Dorier, with Bill Strait and me credited as co-authors. I updated and extended explanations for the second community edition and brought the book into GitHub for community development.
Author credits · My edition foreword
Pay To EndPoint (P2EP)
I co-proposed P2EP and published its design explanation in July 2018 after a group discussion. It explores collaboration between sender and receiver during a payment to disrupt common-input ownership assumptions. Nicolas Dorier’s later BIP78 acknowledges my implementation feedback.
BIP78: A Simple Payjoin Proposal

Dumplings
I created this research tool in 2020 for reproducible CoinJoin statistics from Bitcoin blockchain data, separating fresh coins from remixes and examining change and post-mix consolidation. Its Sake command derives simulation parameters from blockchain data.
Sake
CoinJoin denomination and amount-decomposition experiments, measuring equal outputs, change, fees, and block-space efficiency. I began the project in December 2021, with later contributions from the team. My Wasabi 2.0 launch log compares mainnet output decompositions with these simulations.
June 2022 launch log
